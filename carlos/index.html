How to use this restart

Four training days, with Day 3 kept easy and Day 4 optional recovery. Start Week 1, Day 1. Move sessions around FedEx shifts; avoid stacking missed sessions. Strength estimates anchor the plan, while training tolerance rebuilds.

Weights: barbell loads include the bar; dumbbell loads are per hand. Listed loads are targets, not minimums. Repetitions in reserve means additional good repetitions you could perform before failure.

Progress only when recovered. Keep 3-4 repetitions available on accessories throughout the cycle. Stop sets before technique changes.

Accessory progression & workload adjustments
  • Repeat a comfortable dose for at least two exposures. Confirm no worsening later that day, next morning, or at the next work shift.
  • Add repetitions first. Rows progress from 10-12 toward 15; incline press from 10 toward 12; curls from 12 toward 15; pushdowns from 12 toward 15. Once the top is comfortable for two exposures, use the smallest weight increase and return to the lower repetition target.
  • A 5 lb dumbbell increase may be too large for curls. Keep the existing weight or use smaller increments. No automatic weekly accessory increases and no forced slow negatives.
  • Weeks 4 and 8: halve accessory sets, rounding down to one where applicable. Week 11: one easy set per selected accessory. Week 12: accessories optional.
  • After a demanding shift or poor recovery, omit optional arms and conditioning first. If warm-ups also feel unusually difficult, reduce main-lift load 5-10%, rounded down to an available increment, or remove a working set.
  • Repeat or reduce a planned load if effort exceeds the week's target. Do not increase load and volume together. No missed or forced repetitions.
Elbow symptoms & when to get help

Strengthening may improve capacity, but does not establish the cause of elbow discomfort or guarantee that other muscles will take its load. This plan is general training, not injury rehabilitation.

Stop a provoking exercise for sharp or increasing pain, altered technique, or loss of strength. If symptoms are worse the next morning or at work, reduce or omit that exercise next time.

Recurring symptoms with parcel handling, especially lasting several weeks or affecting work, warrant clinician or physical therapist assessment. Seek prompt medical care for new numbness, marked weakness, significant swelling, or an acute snapping injury. Severe pain with difficulty moving, deformity, or a hot swollen elbow with fever needs urgent assessment.
